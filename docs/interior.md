# Experimental 3D enthalpy component

`src/simulation/interior.ts` is a CPU, double-precision, Cartesian, fixed-grid **thermal experiment**, not the coupled Europa solver. It solves `∂H/∂t = ∇·(k∇T) + q` by explicit finite volumes. Enthalpy `H` is in J/m³ and is referenced to solid ice at its melting temperature. Below melting, `H = ρ cᵢ (T − Tₘ)`; the latent interval is `0 ≤ H ≤ ρL` with liquid fraction `H/(ρL)`; above it, `H = ρL + ρ c𝓌 (T − Tₘ)`. The density is constant across phases. Cells conduct through shared faces, with harmonic-mean conductivity and equal-and-opposite energy exchanges. The four side boundaries are insulated. At top and bottom, prescribed flux is **positive out of the domain**, in W/m². Heat sources are in W/m³. SI units are used throughout.

`createInteriorState` constructs a uniform starting condition. `stepInterior` returns a new enthalpy array without mutating the old one, rejects a timestep above `maximumInteriorTimeStep`, and rejects invalid or nonphysical states. `interiorDiagnostics` reports domain enthalpy, mean liquid fraction and temperature extrema. The explicit stability limit uses the largest conductivity and smallest heat capacity across both phases; source terms and boundary fluxes can still require a smaller timestep.

The separate WebGPU thermal experiment uses float32 arithmetic and independently advances the CPU float64 reference at the same timestep. Every published step checks all cells: maximum temperature error must be at most 0.05 K, maximum liquid-fraction error at most 0.002, and the integrated enthalpy error divided by the largest of latent energy for the domain, the integral of the absolute reference enthalpy, and 1 J must be at most 0.0001. This scale also checks that the reference energy agrees with initial energy plus cumulative heat when a checkpoint is restored. It does not depend on potentially cancelling historical accounting values. An invalid result, lost device or failed comparison stops the run before replacing the last accepted state. Checkpoint data include both states and energy-accounting information, but imported values and their origins remain unverified; consistency checks and checksums do not authenticate them. The default 16×16×16 grid and all its material and forcing values are **synthetic test inputs**, not observations or estimates of Europa. Thermal and orbital experiments do not exchange heat or forces. These limits are comparison tolerances, not a validation of the physical model. Hardware GPU testing is needed in addition to automated tests using a mock device.

Older thermal checkpoints are rechecked under the current scale on import. A checksum-valid file can therefore be rejected if it no longer meets the energy-accounting or GPU comparison limit.

The test suite checks energy conservation and the signs of boundary fluxes, a linear steady-conduction solution, grid refinement against an analytical transient heat-equation eigenmode, isothermal latent-energy accounting, phase/enthalpy round trips, and invalid inputs. These are component checks, **not** an analytical Stefan front benchmark or validation against Europa observations. There is no spherical geometry, orbit feedback, viscoelastic solid, fluid motion, self-gravity, resolved moving interface, or realistic phase-dependent density. A volume fraction is not a resolved shell boundary. Input parameters and any heat source must not be described as measured Europa quantities unless separately sourced. Before research use, implement the missing physics, establish grid/timestep convergence for coupled simulations, and compare against published analytical Stefan and other applicable benchmarks.
