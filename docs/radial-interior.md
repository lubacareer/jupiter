# Independent radial ice–ocean experiment

This is an **unvalidated research prototype**, not an inferred interior of Europa or a coupled tidal model. It solves a one-dimensional, spherically symmetric enthalpy diffusion problem in a water/ice layer above a fixed rocky boundary. There is no ocean flow, salinity, variable pressure, rock thermodynamics, viscoelastic heating, self-gravity, changing geometry, or feedback to orbital motion. Temperature and liquid fraction are constant on a spherical shell. The cutaway maps the radial field onto a 3D illustration; that projection is not a 3D simulation.

## Equations and inputs

Volumetric enthalpy `H` (J/m³) is measured relative to solid ice at the prescribed melting temperature. The constitutive phase law is the same as the Cartesian experiment in [interior.md](interior.md). The model solves `∂H/∂t = r⁻² ∂/∂r(r² k ∂T/∂r)` using explicit, cell-centred finite volumes. Conductivities vary linearly with liquid fraction; the harmonic mean applies at internal faces. Shared-face heat transfer is equal and opposite. The outer boundary holds a constant temperature at the surface; a positive basal flux adds heat at the inner boundary. There is no volumetric source.

Every cell's true spherical volume and face areas are used. The explicit timestep limit uses the largest material conductivity, smallest phase heat capacity, geometry, and the half-cell distance at the fixed-temperature surface. The default timestep is 45% of this bound. A smaller timestep can still be needed when forcing would create negative absolute temperature. Energy accounting integrates the actual inner and outer boundary transfers, and is normalized using the largest of 1 J, integrated absolute enthalpy and the latent energy of the domain. A restored state failing the 0.0001 accounting threshold is rejected; agreement is a consistency check, not authentication or validation.

The starting radius of 1,560.8 km is from [JPL's satellite physical parameters](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/phys_par/). Starting ice thickness 20 km and ocean depth 100 km are illustrative values within the approximate ranges in [NASA's Europa Clipper FAQ](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/europa-clipper/mission-faq/). The initial solid shell temperature changes linearly from 100 K at the surface to 273.15 K at its base; the initial ocean is liquid at 273.15 K. The 100 K temperature and 0.05 W/m² basal heat flux are selected assumptions, not measured global values. The default grid has 120 radial cells. The half-liquid crossing defines the displayed ice thickness only when the surface side is predominantly solid, the bottom is liquid, and exactly one crossing exists; otherwise no unique transition is reported.

Constant density 921 kg/m³, ice heat capacity 2,040 J/(kg K), water heat capacity 4,180 J/(kg K), ice conductivity 2.2 W/(m K), liquid conductivity 0.57 W/(m K), and latent heat 333,800 J/kg are approximate pure-water properties near melting, based on the [NREL material properties appendix](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy03osti/33954.pdf). They are used unchanged at colder temperatures and for both phase densities; this simplification and omission of salt and pressure substantially limit interpretation. Inputs are not a calibrated Europa material profile.

## Numerical checks

The automated tests include a steady spherical conduction profile with `T(r) = T_surface + q_b r_inner² / k · (1/r − 1/R)`, 150 K surface temperature, 0.001 W/m² basal flux, and pure ice in the default 120 km layer. At 30, 60, and 120 cells, the relative discrepancy between outgoing and incoming boundary heat in one step is approximately 0.0012830, 0.0006411, and 0.0003205 respectively. This checks convergence of spherical geometry and boundary fluxes, not Europa's real heat flow.

A phase-change test begins with liquid water at 273.15 K under a 263.15 K boundary, no basal flux, a 20 m layer, and radius 10⁹ m (the locally planar limit). At 28,512,974.65 s, the one-phase Stefan similarity solution gives a 2 m freezing front. The numerical half-liquid front is approximately 1.7800, 1.8755, and 1.9205 m on 32, 64, and 128 cells respectively: errors decrease from 0.2200 to 0.0795 m. The analytical one-phase Stefan comparison is a standard phase-change benchmark; see [Esen and Kutluay](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0096-3003(02)00846-9). Finite thickness, the phase-law discretization, and spherical curvature make this an approximation to that analytical limit.

Tests additionally check integrated energy accounting, invalid inputs, and ambiguous front handling. The half-liquid depth is mesh-dependent and is not a separately tracked sharp Stefan boundary. These checks do not validate the omitted planetary physics.
